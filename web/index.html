So what is the catch?

Four things it
cannot do.

Every app's landing page lists what it can do. This is the other half, up here rather than buried, so nothing about it is a surprise after you install it.

  1. It cannot read a protected screen.

    Banking apps and DRM video mark their windows secure, and Android hands CopyEye a blank image instead of their contents. It says so and stops. There is no setting that gets around this, and there will not be one.

  2. It cannot skip Android's permission dialog.

    That prompt belongs to the system, not to us, and it cannot be remembered forever. You choose which way to trade: see it once per session and keep a recording icon, or see it per scan and keep your status bar clean.

  3. It cannot switch on your OEM's own setting.

    Xiaomi, Oppo, Vivo and Realme block apps from opening over other apps until you allow it in their security app. CopyEye detects when this has happened and points at the exact screen — but you have to flip it.

  4. It cannot read what it cannot see.

    It works on pixels, not meaning. Tiny, blurred or heavily stylised text comes back wrong sometimes. Zooming in before you scan fixes most of it.

Where does my screen go?

It has no way
to send anything
anywhere.

Most apps promise not to upload your data. CopyEye cannot. It declares no internet permission, so the operating system will not give it a network connection — not for analytics, not for crash reports, not by accident in a future build.

  • Recognition runs on your phone. Both language models ship inside the app. It works in flight mode.
  • Nothing is saved. The captured frame lives in memory and is destroyed when you close the scan. Never the gallery, never a file.
  • Nothing is watched. Between taps there is no capture session at all, so no frame of your screen is even produced.
  • No camera. No microphone. No location. None of it is requested, because none of it is needed.

Does it read Hindi?

A Hindi caption over an English interface is not an edge case here — it is the normal case. Both recognisers run on the same frame, and the English is selectable while the Hindi is still arriving.

Questions

Before you install.

Can it really copy text from a video?

Yes. CopyEye reads the frame that is on screen the moment you tap, so pausing on the frame you want gives the cleanest result. Reels, Shorts, YouTube, a downloaded film — they are all just pixels, and pixels are what it reads.

Does it work without internet?

Completely. Both language models are inside the app. There is no first-run download and no server involved, which is why the app can declare no internet permission at all.

Why does my phone show a screen-recording icon?

Android shows that indicator whenever an app holds screen-capture permission, and no app is permitted to hide it. CopyEye only reads your screen in the moment you tap, and you can set it to hand the permission straight back afterwards so the icon disappears between scans — at the cost of the system asking again next time.

I tapped the eye and nothing happened.

On Xiaomi, Redmi, POCO, Oppo, Vivo and Realme phones there is a second permission beyond "display over other apps". Turn on Display pop-up windows while running in background in your phone's app settings. CopyEye now detects this and shows you the exact path.

Is it on the Play Store?

Not yet. The APK here is signed and installs directly. Your phone will warn you about installing outside the store — that warning is normal for any direct download.
